## 0. TL;DR for the build team

- Most Indian market-abuse cases follow a short list of patterns: insider trading, front-running, pump-and-dump, circular or reversal trading, and unregistered advice. The exchanges already send brokers 15 named alert types, including "Front Running", "Pump and Dump", "Wash Sales", "Circular Trading" and "Order book spoofing" ([Rupeezy surveillance policy reproducing the BSE/NSE list](https://static.rupeezy.in/cms/file_resource/2025/06/11/Surveillance_obligation_of_TM.pdf)). A demo that recreates those alerts and adds evidence, a clause citation and a human-review step lines up with what regulators already ask brokers to do.
- SEBI's own orders describe its methods, and those methods can be rebuilt: time-sliced "patch" analysis of cash and options (Jane Street) ([SEBI PDF](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/jul-2025/1751584518593.pdf)), WhatsApp chats and call records (Axis MF) ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/corporate/story/whatsapp-chats-to-call-records-how-sebi-pieced-together-the-axis-mutual-fund-front-running-case-545159-2026-07-24)), and the timing of broadcasts compared with trades (Sanjiv Bhasin) ([SEBI PDF](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/jun-2025/sb_recommendation_interim_order.pdf)).
- The hackathon bans customer data (per the user brief). Use public market data you download by hand (NSE bhavcopy, PIT disclosures, SEBI orders) plus synthetic clients, orders and chats. NSE's terms of use prohibit automated scraping ([NSE Terms of Use](https://www.nseindia.com/static/nse-terms-of-use)).
